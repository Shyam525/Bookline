# Bookline Provider Discovery & Field Interview Guide

> **Note for Founder / Interviewer**:  
> This interview guide is a structured discovery tool designed to gather empirical, unvarnished insights from local service business owners in Gujarat. **Do not lead the witness or pitch Bookline during the first half of the interview.** Do not treat these questions as marketing; treat them as an operational audit to uncover actual customer behavior, scheduling pain points, and offline realities.

---

## 1. Interview Goals & Target Profile

### Target Business Profiles
- **Segment A**: High-traffic unisex and women's salons (3–8 styling chairs, 2–5 estheticians).
- **Segment B**: Traditional and modern men's barbershops / grooming lounges (2–5 chairs).
- **Segment C**: Independent beauty studios / bridal makeup artists / nail bars.
- **Segment D**: Day spas and wellness centers.

### Target Locations in Gujarat
- **Ahmedabad**: Prahlad Nagar, Bodakdev, Satellite, CG Road, Chandkheda, Navrangpura.
- **Surat**: Vesu, Adajan, Piplod, Ghod Dod Road.
- **Vadodara**: Alkapuri, Gotri, Vasna Road.
- **Rajkot**: Kalawad Road, Yagnik Road, Amin Marg.

## 2. Interview Structure & Timing (30–40 Minutes Total)

```
Part 1: Background & Scale         (5 mins)  ──► Understand operational context
Part 2: Current Booking Workflow   (10 mins) ──► Map the appointment lifecycle
Part 3: Conflict & Double-Booking  (10 mins) ──► Uncover real pain and friction
Part 4: Financials & Offline Pay   (5 mins)  ──► Understand payment & commission reality
Part 5: Technology & Language      (5 mins)  ──► Assess UI comfort and Gujarati need
Part 6: Willingness to Pay & Next  (5 mins)  ──► Validate SaaS subscription hypothesis
```

---

## 3. Detailed Question Script

### Part 1: Background & Daily Operations
1. "How many chairs or treatment rooms do you have operating on a typical Saturday?"
2. "How many staff members work here full-time vs part-time?"
3. "Who typically manages customer arrivals during rush hours — yourself, a receptionist, or individual stylists?"
4. "What are your busiest hours of the week, and how do you handle customer wait times during peak rush?"

### Part 2: Current Booking & Appointment Handling
1. "When a customer wants an appointment, how do they usually reach you? (Walk-in, phone call, WhatsApp message, Instagram DM?)"
2. "How do you record that appointment right now? (Paper diary, wall whiteboard, Google Calendar, memory, salon software?)"
3. "If using software or a paper diary, what do you dislike most about it?"
4. "How do you ensure two stylists aren't double-booked for the same time or same station?"
5. "What happens when a walk-in walks in at 4:00 PM, but a customer called earlier asking for 4:15 PM?"

### Part 3: Friction, No-Shows & Operational Chaos
1. "How often do customers fail to show up for an appointment without calling in advance?"
2. "How do you remind clients about upcoming bookings today? Does someone message them manually on WhatsApp?"
3. "Have you ever had a customer show up claiming they booked an appointment with a specific stylist, but that stylist was already busy or absent? How did you resolve it?"
4. "How do you manage stylist lunch breaks, personal time-off, or unexpected absences?"

### Part 4: Payments & Cash/UPI Handling
1. "What percentage of your daily payments are made in Cash vs UPI (GPay/PhonePe/Paytm QR) vs Card?"
2. "Do you ask for advance deposits for high-value services (like bridal makeup, keratin, hair coloring), or do clients pay 100% after service completion?"
3. "How do you calculate your daily revenue at the end of the day?"
4. "How do you pay your stylists (fixed salary, pure commission, or salary plus service commission)?"

### Part 5: Language, Staff Literacy & Technology
1. "What language are your stylists most comfortable communicating in daily? (Gujarati, Hindi, English?)"
2. "If salon management software were in Gujarati, would your receptionists or junior staff find it easier to use than an English-only system?"
3. "Do you primarily manage your business from a smartphone, a tablet on the front desk, or a desktop computer?"

### Part 6: Subscription Hypothesis & Willingness to Pay
1. "If a single tool allowed you to share a direct booking link on your Instagram and WhatsApp status, automatically stopped double-bookings, and let you manage your salon calendar from your phone, would you consider paying a monthly subscription for it?"
2. "What monthly software price range feels reasonable for an independent salon like yours: ₹500/month, ₹1,000/month, or ₹2,000/month?"
3. "Would you be open to trialing a simple, clean booking tool for 2 weeks in exchange for your feedback?"
